### Andrej Karpathy

Andrej Karpathy, former Director of AI at Tesla and founding member of OpenAI, shared a simple but powerful tip: ask your LLM to "structure your response as HTML" at the end of a query, then view the generated file in your browser. He argues this is the next evolution beyond raw text and markdown — HTML gives you far more flexibility in graphics, layout, and even interactivity. His broader thesis: audio is the human-preferred input to AIs, but vision (images, animations, video) is the preferred output. Roughly a third of our brains are dedicated to visual processing, making it the "10-lane superhighway" into the brain. He predicts the end state is interactive neural videos generated directly by diffusion models, though there's still a long way to go before we get there.

### No Priors — Amex Global Business Travel: The World's First AI Take Private with Long Lake CEO Alexander Taubman

**The Takeaway:** The most effective way to deploy AI into the real economy isn't selling software — it's buying the companies themselves.

Alexander Taubman, cofounder and CEO of Long Lake Management, just announced a $6.3 billion deal to acquire American Express Global Business Travel — what he believes is the world's first AI take-private. Long Lake has already done 30 acquisitions under a radical premise: buy traditional service businesses, deploy a horizontal AI platform called Nexus, and transform them from within.

Taubman's core insight is contrarian: most companies struggle to adopt AI not because the technology doesn't work, but because of change management. "When you're just selling software and you don't actually then care what happens with the business outcomes, you just don't see the same business outcomes," he says. By owning the companies, Long Lake creates a tight feedback loop — engineers sit in the field with employees, understand pain points, and build tools within Nexus to solve them.

The results are striking. Long Lake's portfolio companies were typically growing 0-5% annually before acquisition. After deploying Nexus, they're growing 20%+. Employees become dramatically more productive — Taubman estimates 30-40% efficiency gains — which creates a powerful talent moat. "If you now leave Long Lake to go to a competitor, you have to start doing all this mundane work again. 25% of your day, 30% of your day, you have to go do that again. The thought of it's like giving up email."

Taubman's philosophy is long-term, modeled after Berkshire Hathaway and Danaher. "You're going to build the best company in the industry, and then you're going to sell it? That just doesn't make sense to me. I'd want to own that company forever and compound on that advantage for decades." He sees AI as deeply positive-sum: "We actually think AI makes people more productive. And when you have more productive people, you want more of them. When your customers are happier, you grow faster. You actually create jobs, and everybody wins."

The takeaway for anyone building in AI: the biggest bottleneck isn't model capability — it's the messy, human work of deployment. And the best way to capture that value might be to stop selling tools and start owning outcomes.
