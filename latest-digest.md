The conversation kicks off with the biggest change since December: coding agents are finally working at longer time horizons. Ari notes that engineers are shifting from individual contributors to "managers of agents" — context-switching between multiple autonomous coding agents. But he's quick to add a dose of realism: "It makes you a lot more productive in some ways... but now you have this pretty massive understanding gap and it's a lot easier to put swag into your code base." The bottleneck just shifts from writing code to reviewing it.

Rob drops a provocative thesis: open-weight AI might be falling off the frontier. Meta appears to be pulling back from open source, and Chinese labs like Qwen and DeepSeek are now keeping their best models proprietary behind APIs. "There are strong indications that they may also be pulling back," Rob says. Ari agrees, predicting we've hit "peak open models" — the financial incentives just don't make sense once you've established credibility.

The most surprising prediction comes from Ari: "It is not hard to imagine a world in which Anthropic is so compute constrained that they actually cut off the API." The labs are already selling futures for guaranteed inference tokens — a sign that compute scarcity is reshaping the business model. Rob adds that labs might not even make their most powerful models available publicly, reserving them for internal use.

On the OpenAI vs. Anthropic vibe shift, Rob doubles down on his spicy prediction from December: Sam Altman might not be CEO by year-end. He floats Bret Taylor (OpenAI's chairman, CEO of Sierra) as a plausible successor — "I think it would honestly make so much sense for OpenAI to acquire Sierra and to make Bret the CEO."

The episode closes with rapid-fire predictions: Ari bets APIs get suspended or heavily limited within 18 months. Rob predicts Anthropic will become a "fledgling juggernaut in the making in life sciences" — their next big bet after coding. And Ari pushes back on the "permanent underclass" narrative: "Humans are slow at dissipating things through the economy... it's gonna take a long time."
