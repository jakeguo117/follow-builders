### Unsupervised Learning — Ep 91: Top AI Analyst Unpacks Today's AI Hype Cycle

**The Takeaway:** The biggest unknown in AI isn't model capabilities — it's whether we can figure out what to actually do with them, and that's a problem no amount of compute can solve.

Benedict Evans, one of tech's most respected independent analysts, sat down with Redpoint's Jacob Efron for a conversation that cuts through the hype with historical perspective. Evans argues that while AI is clearly a big deal, the way we talk about it often falls into logical fallacies and unfalsifiable claims.

His core framework is refreshingly grounded: look at what actually happened with previous platform shifts. Mobile networks saw traffic increase 2,000x and spent $200B/year on CapEx — and still didn't capture most of the value. That went to Uber, YouTube, and everyone else up the stack. Evans suggests AI might follow a similar pattern where the model layer itself doesn't capture the lion's share of value.

On the job displacement question, Evans is skeptical of dramatic predictions. He points out that we've been "automating higher and higher level human functions" for centuries, and each time, new jobs emerged that nobody could have predicted. "You can always see the jobs that will go away, and you don't know the new jobs," he says. The real challenge isn't model capability — it's that most people aren't tool builders, don't see the problems that could be solved, and aren't in a position to build solutions even if they did.

One of his sharpest observations: coding works brilliantly for AI because it's verifiable at scale — you can run tests millions of times. But the further you get from something tangible and objective, the harder it becomes. "Where do you not want the average?" he asks. "In principle, what these models are doing is asking 'how would most people probably do this?' But sometimes you don't want the average — you want something completely different."

**Memorable quote:** "It's like looking at the internet in 1995 and saying 'but it's just random people talking to each other on chat rooms.' It's not the chat rooms — it's the network."
