### No Priors — The Rise of the Full-Stack Builder and Hyper-Leveraged Generalist with Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella

**The Takeaway:** The most important shift in AI isn't the models themselves — it's the ecosystem and harness around them that determines who captures value.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella sat down with No Priors (hosted by Sarah Guo and Elad Gil, with Swyx as co-host) for a wide-ranging conversation at Microsoft Build. The core argument: AI is an ecosystem play, not a single-model or single-platform game. Microsoft's strategy is to build the platform and tooling that lets *every* company — not just AI-native startups — participate as a first-class citizen in the AI economy.

The most provocative idea Nadella surfaces is that **private evals may become a company's biggest IP**. "What's that private eval that you can then use, even a frontier model, hill climb on and not leak the traces?" he asks. "If you can switch from model A to model B and climb up, you're in control. If you can't, you're not." This reframes the entire debate about moats in AI — it's not the model, it's the evaluation infrastructure you build around it.

On the future of work, Nadella offers a concrete example from Microsoft's own Azure networking team. Faced with unprecedented scaling demands (building more Azure capacity in 15 months than the first 15 years), the team reconceptualized their job: "Our job is not to do Azure networking. Our job is to build the agentic system that does Azure networking." They built an agent called Miles, and now they're "screaming for more tokens" instead of more headcount.

On the SaaS debate, Nadella is nuanced. He believes the data models underneath SaaS applications are durable — "general ledger better be a general ledger" — but the packaging and business models will need to unbundle and rebundle. He predicts per-user pricing will persist alongside consumption-based pricing, and that outcome-based pricing, while appealing in theory, often backfires when customers realize they're sharing their upside.

Perhaps the most quotable moment: "The world is going to be very skeptical of tech and tech companies that say, 'Trust us. We've got it. The future is going to be glorious.' You kinda have to deliver tangible benefits because it's too important this time around. It's too much of the economy for it not to be the case."

Nadella also reflects on what he's personally building — long-running Foundry agents, including a chief-of-staff autopilot he built in a weekend using WorkIQ. "Building software has made it possible for even the incompetence of a CEO of a company like ours you can build," he jokes, before making a serious point: the rise of the full-stack builder and hyper-leveraged generalist is where the maximum returns will be.
