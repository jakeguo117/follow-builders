## PODCASTS

**No Priors — Why Traditional Benchmarks Fail Modern AI Models with OpenAI Research Scientist Noam Brown**

**The Takeaway:** The way the industry evaluates AI models is fundamentally broken — and the safety frameworks that govern model releases haven't caught up.

OpenAI research scientist Noam Brown, one of the godfathers of AI reasoning, argues that current benchmarks are misleading because they don't control for test-time compute. With GPT-3, you couldn't scale test-time compute — giving it $10 million wouldn't help much. But today's models like GPT-5.5 can think productively for weeks. Brown's core point: "The capability of the model is a function of how much money you put into it." A model with a $10,000 budget can do vastly more than one with a $10 budget — and existing safety policies don't account for this.

The implications are profound. Brown notes that safety evaluations (responsible scaling policies, preparedness frameworks) were developed before test-time compute scaling was a thing. They ask "what can the model do?" without asking "at what budget?" This means dangerous capabilities could be hiding in plain sight — accessible only to those willing to spend enough.

Brown shared a personal example: he uses GPT-5.5 to build poker bots. With 5.2, he could build a reverse solver about 5x faster than alone, but the model would gaslight him — claiming $92 when the answer was $100. With 5.5, it can do zero-shot what used to require hours of steering. He predicts that within 6-12 months, the model will be able to reproduce his entire PhD thesis in one go.

On recursive self-improvement, Brown is measured: "I don't think we're close to a very fast takeoff." Time itself is the bottleneck — models need to run for long enough to do something truly powerful. The biggest bottleneck for all researchers right now is time.

His contrarian take: even though test-time compute scaling is now widely accepted in theory, the industry is stuck in a "bad equilibrium" where everyone publishes benchmark grids without an x-axis for compute budget. He wrote his essay to break this equilibrium.

> "If you give it a budget of $10,000, it can do a lot more than what it can do with a budget of $10. Give it a budget of $10,000,000, it can do even more. At what budget should you evaluate these models? The policies that exist today don't really address that question."

**一句话总结：** 行业评估 AI 模型的方式从根本上就是错的——而且管理模型发布的安全框架也还没跟上。

OpenAI 研究科学家、AI 推理领域的教父之一 Noam Brown 认为，当前的基准测试具有误导性，因为它们没有控制 test-time compute。GPT-3 时代，你无法扩展 test-time compute——给它 1000 万美元也没太大帮助。但今天的模型如 GPT-5.5 可以高效地思考数周。Brown 的核心观点是："模型的能力基本上取决于你投入多少钱。"预算 1 万美元的模型能做的远比预算 10 美元的多得多——而现有的安全政策没有考虑到这一点。

其影响是深远的。Brown 指出，安全评估（负责任扩展政策、准备框架）都是在 test-time compute 扩展成为现实之前制定的。它们问的是"模型能做什么？"而没有问"在什么预算下？"这意味着危险能力可能就藏在眼前——只有那些愿意投入足够多的人才能触及。

Brown 分享了一个个人例子：他用 GPT-5.5 构建扑克机器人。用 5.2，他构建一个反向求解器的速度比单独做快约 5 倍，但模型会 gaslight 他——答案明明是 100 美元，它却说 92 美元。而用 5.5，它可以零样本完成以前需要数小时引导的工作。他预测在 6-12 个月内，模型将能一次复现他的整个博士论文。

关于递归自我改进，Brown 保持克制："我认为我们离非常快速的起飞还很远。"时间本身就是瓶颈——模型需要运行足够长的时间才能做真正强大的事情。目前所有研究人员最大的瓶颈就是时间。

他的逆向观点是：尽管 test-time compute 扩展在理论上已被广泛接受，但行业陷入了一个"坏均衡"——每个人都在发布没有计算预算 x 轴的基准网格。他写那篇文章就是为了打破这个均衡。

> "如果你给它 1 万美元的预算，它能做的远多于给它 10 美元预算。给它 1000 万美元，它能做得更多。应该在什么预算下评估这些模型？现有的政策根本没有解决这个问题。"
