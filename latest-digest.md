### Box CEO Aaron Levie
Aaron Levie weighed in on Anthropic's economics research, agreeing that AI has been less disruptive to jobs than expected. His take: AI still requires humans to produce value in most cases. It automates specific tasks, not entire jobs — and when you automate tasks, you often increase output, raising (or maintaining) demand for that role. He invoked Jevons paradox: as AI makes work cheaper and faster, demand for that work actually grows. He sees this clearly in software engineering, where companies of all sizes can now take on projects that were previously impractical.

https://x.com/levie/status/2080156917373214900

Box CEO Aaron Levie 评论了 Anthropic 的经济学研究，同意 AI 对就业的冲击比预期要小。他的观点：AI 在大多数情况下仍然需要人来产生价值。它自动化的是特定任务，而不是整个工作——当你自动化任务时，产出反而会增加，从而维持甚至提升对该岗位的需求。他引用了杰文斯悖论：当 AI 让工作变得更便宜、更快时，对这项工作的需求反而会增长。他在软件工程领域清楚地看到了这一点——各种规模的公司现在都能承接以前不切实际的项目。

### AI & I by Every — "How Every's Team Used AI to Ship Its Biggest Launch Ever"

**The Takeaway:** When you fully commit to an AI-native workflow — where agents handle execution from idea to shipping — a single prompt can generate $25,000 in revenue while you're at the gym.

Every launched the "Builder Pack," a bundle of premium AI tool credits (Codex, Cursor, Claude, Gemini, Framer, Render, PostHog, Notion, and more) as part of their new All Access tier ($625/year). But the real story isn't the product — it's how the team built and shipped it using the very tools in the pack.

The episode features a roundtable with the Every team. Yash, who runs Sparkle and growth, describes automating his entire A/B testing pipeline with Claude: "I spent one month doing these things, and I'm like, okay. I'm done. I wanna do other things now." He's moved from execution to ideas — deciding what to test rather than setting up experiments.

Austin, a growth team member, tells the most striking story. He took a screenshot of a Slack thread where the team discussed retargeting users who had shown checkout intent during the Builder Pack launch. He dropped it into Codex with a "slash LFG" command — the team's compound engineering harness — and went to the gym. When he came back, Codex had: created four audience cohorts in Kit, drafted targeted emails for each, run the copy through Spiral (their AI writing tool) for brand-consistent tone, analyzed past sends for optimal language and imagery, and scheduled everything for the next morning. Those emails drove over $25,000 in revenue.

"I wasn't like, holy shit, that's so cool. I was like, that's basically what it should do. My brain is so cooked on this stuff now," Austin said.

Douglas, the creative director, frames the shift as moving from "playing an instrument to conducting an orchestra." He uses Flora (via MCP) for design work, feeding it competitive audits and mood boards generated by Claude. The Builder Pack's green branding — a deliberate departure from Every's usual aesthetic — came from this workflow.

The episode closes with practical advice for new builders: dupe a product you love, rebuild your portfolio, or just start with a simple app you're excited to text a friend about. The tools will figure out the rest.

**核心要点：** 当你完全拥抱 AI 原生工作流——让 agent 处理从想法到交付的全流程——一条 prompt 就能在你健身的时候带来 25,000 美元的收入。

Every 推出了"Builder Pack"，这是一个捆绑了多个优质 AI 工具积分（Codex、Cursor、Claude、Gemini、Framer、Render、PostHog、Notion 等）的产品包，作为他们新的 All Access 订阅层级（每年 625 美元）的一部分。但真正的故事不是产品本身——而是团队如何用包里的工具来构建和交付这个产品。

Austin 说："我当时的反应不是'卧槽太酷了'，而是'这不就是它该做的吗'。我的脑子已经被这些东西搞坏了。"
