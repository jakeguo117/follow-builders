**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — OpenAI's Dan Roberts: Why AI Can Now Make Discoveries**

**The Takeaway: Reinforcement learning isn't just the cherry on top anymore — it's the cake itself, and it's what enabled AI to solve unsolved math problems by thinking for hours in ways humans never could.**

Dan Roberts, lead of the Foundations of Reinforcement Learning team at OpenAI, comes from a background in theoretical physics (PhD from MIT, postdoc at the Institute for Advanced Study). He wrote a book on applying physics tools to understand deep learning, and joined OpenAI two years ago because the most exciting scientific progress was happening at the frontier.

The conversation centers on the recent breakthrough where OpenAI's models solved unsolved math problems (the Erdős problems). Roberts explains the key insight: "One of the things that ChatGPT was able to do was assume it was false. When you go against the grain and do something contrarian like that, you really have to have strong conviction in what you're doing in order to persevere down a really long calculation path." The model spent hours exploring different approaches, ultimately disproving a conjecture everyone believed was true — and did so by connecting two disparate fields of mathematics.

Roberts draws a clear distinction between two approaches to AI math: DeepMind's method uses formal languages (Lean) to produce airtight proofs, while OpenAI's approach works in informal language, much like a human mathematician would. He argues the informal approach is more scalable because you don't need to formalize every problem first.

On RL itself, Roberts gives a vivid analogy: supervised learning is like watching your dad play Mario without ever touching the controller; RL is actually playing yourself, making mistakes, and learning from feedback. The catch? When rewards are sparse — like a chess game where you only know if you won at the very end — RL becomes extremely hard because you can't tell which of your many actions were good or bad.

Roberts makes a bold claim: RL is no longer the cherry on top of the cake — it is the cake. He argues that with powerful enough pretrained models, RL unlocks test-time reasoning that fundamentally changes what models can do. On the famous "sucking supervision through a straw" critique (less than 1 bit of useful information per 10,000 tokens), he counters that the method clearly produces breakthroughs in coding and science that wouldn't be possible otherwise.

Perhaps most provocatively, Roberts argues against the "scale is all you need" narrative: "It's not that scale is all you need. You need to also have good ideas to guide the scaling." He advocates for a physics-inspired approach: when something "emerges" at scale, don't call it emergence — go back and build simpler models that reproduce the phenomenon, so you truly understand it. "There's no grokking," he says. "There's just this nice continuity."

Looking ahead, Roberts is cautious about predicting when AI will do fully autonomous science. But he's genuinely thrilled: "I feel really excited that we will get to really answer a lot of fundamental questions in the field of science that we care about with the aid or the models being the driving force."

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — OpenAI 的 Dan Roberts：为什么 AI 现在能做出科学发现**

**核心观点：强化学习不再是蛋糕上的樱桃——它本身就是蛋糕。正是 RL 让 AI 能够以人类无法做到的方式思考数小时，解决未解的数学难题。**

Dan Roberts 是 OpenAI 强化学习基础团队的负责人，拥有理论物理学背景（MIT 博士，普林斯顿高等研究院博士后）。他写过一本关于如何运用物理学工具理解深度学习的书，两年前加入 OpenAI，因为最激动人心的科学进展正发生在前沿。

对话围绕最近 OpenAI 模型解决未解数学难题（Erdős 问题）的突破展开。Roberts 解释了关键洞察："ChatGPT 能做到的一件事是假设它是错的。当你反其道而行之时，你真的需要有强烈的信念才能坚持走完一条漫长的计算路径。"模型花了数小时探索不同方法，最终推翻了一个所有人都相信为真的猜想——而且是通过连接两个截然不同的数学领域做到的。

Roberts 清晰区分了 AI 数学的两种方法：DeepMind 使用形式化语言（Lean）生成无可挑剔的证明，而 OpenAI 的方法则使用非形式化语言，更像人类数学家的做法。他认为非形式化方法更具可扩展性，因为你不需要先形式化每个问题。

关于 RL 本身，Roberts 给出了一个生动的类比：监督学习就像看着你爸爸玩马里奥，自己从未碰过手柄；RL 则是亲自上手玩，犯错，然后从反馈中学习。问题在于？当奖励很稀疏时——比如一盘棋，你只有到最后才知道输赢——RL 变得极其困难，因为你无法判断众多动作中哪些是好的、哪些是坏的。

Roberts 提出了一个大胆的主张：RL 不再是蛋糕上的樱桃——它就是蛋糕本身。他认为，有了足够强大的预训练模型，RL 能够释放 test-time reasoning，从根本上改变模型的能力。针对著名的"每 10,000 个 token 只吸取不到 1 比特有用信息"的批评，他反驳说，这种方法显然在编程和科学领域产生了其他方式无法实现的突破。

也许最具争议的是，Roberts 反对"规模就是一切"的说法："不是规模就是一切。你还需要好的想法来引导规模化。"他倡导一种受物理学启发的方法：当某些东西在规模化后"涌现"时，不要称之为涌现——回去构建能复现该现象的简单模型，这样你才能真正理解它。"没有什么 grokking，"他说，"只有这种美妙的连续性。"

展望未来，Roberts 对 AI 何时能实现完全自主的科学发现持谨慎态度。但他由衷地感到兴奋："我感到非常激动，因为我们终将能够借助 AI——或者说以模型为驱动力——真正回答我们关心的科学领域中的许多基本问题。"
