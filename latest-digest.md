### AI & I by Every — The AI Workflows Behind Every's Consulting Team

**The Takeaway:** The most effective AI adoption isn't about replacing humans — it's about building systems (loops) that let AI execute standard operating procedures while humans provide taste, oversight, and relationship management.

In this episode, Every CEO Dan Shipper chats with Natalia, Every's Head of Consulting, about how her AI workflows have evolved dramatically over the past few months. Natalia manages "Claudie," Every's internal AI agent employee who handles CRM management, sales proposals, and operations — running on a loop that self-evaluates performance and improves based on feedback.

The most counterintuitive insight? Natalia actually *bought* a professional CRM (Attio) and project management tool (Asana) instead of continuing to vibe-code her own. Her reasoning is sharp: "In the era of AI, you can build anything. The question is, should you build and maintain whatever you actually build?" AI is great at executing against standard operating procedures, but maintaining data quality, managing complex logic, and providing taste still require human oversight. As she puts it, "Claudie still needs two things: constant oversight and management, and I want to interface with people."

Natalia's most impressive workflow is her email triage system, built on top of Dan's open-source "Tend" app. She trained it on 150 of her most recent emails, gave it a flowchart of her sales pipeline logic, and now her email "knows what's going on more than I do." She can approve drafts, archive to client-specific markdown files, or convert items to Asana tasks with a single click.

Dan offers a powerful metaphor for this shift: "Knowledge work is turning into gardening. When you're gardening, you're creating the conditions for the growth to happen, but you're not making the plant with your hands." The "loop" is the system that does your work for you — you intervene at the beginning and end, and compound learnings back into the system over time.

Natalia's advice for executives: start with the systems you already have, think about what tasks only humans should do, and begin with small, well-defined tasks. "The single biggest mistake is wanting to remake the whole thing. Often it just means you need to standardize and write down how you do a single thing really well."

She also shared a deeply personal project — a Codex-built app that triages her 81-year-old father's care, pulling in reports from multiple nurses, WhatsApp threads, and medical appointments into a single family portal. It's a reminder that AI's most meaningful impact isn't always about coding faster — it's about taking the administrative load off so humans can focus on being present and loving.
