### Box CEO Aaron Levie

Box CEO Aaron Levie (levie on X) shared his perspective on the rise of advanced AI agents moving from coding into broader knowledge work. He argues that building robust, mission-critical agents is "not a side project or something you can just do on nights and weekends" — it requires ensuring agents have the right context and data, wiring systems safely, designing human-in-the-loop workflows, and maintaining agents through upgrades. As a result, Box is starting to hire for "AI automation engineering" roles, which Levie sees as a forward-deployed engineer role for internal functions. He predicts most companies will have many flavors of this role going forward.

### No Priors — Baseten CEO Tuhin Srivastava on the AI Inference Crunch, Custom Models, and Building the Inference Cloud

**The Takeaway:** The AI inference market is the last market — even after AGI, all that's left is inference — and it's currently in a severe supply crunch that most people don't fully appreciate.

Baseten CEO Tuhin Srivastava joined No Priors to discuss the state of AI inference, a market his company has grown 30x in the past year on track to exceed $1 billion in revenue. Srivastava's core thesis: inference is the ultimate market because it represents the ongoing consumption of intelligence, not just its creation.

The most striking insight is the severity of the compute crunch. Srivastava reveals that Baseten runs clusters at "uncomfortably high utilization" — mid-90s most of the time. The company sits across 18 different clouds with 90 clusters worldwide, yet still struggles to find supply. Getting new capacity now requires 3-5 year contracts with 20-30% prepayment. "There is very, very little slack compute available," he says. "Even when there is capacity available, there's a lot of grifty suppliers who don't understand SLAs for inference. There's probably a dozen good clouds, and I'd put three or four of them in the gold tier."

On the application layer debate — whether independent AI companies can survive against the labs — Srivastava is bullish. His argument: value lies in user signal and workflows that only the application layer can capture. He cites Abridge as an example: the value isn't just in transcription, but in the three-step workflow inside the EMR that happens after the note is taken. "It's very, very hard for a frontier model company to be able to eat up where that could — they just don't have access to that user signal."

A surprising data point: 90-95% of tokens served on Baseten are from custom models, not vanilla open source. "No one is just running the vanilla open source weights," Srivastava says. "You might be customizing it for quality, but you also might be customizing it for performance." This suggests the market has already moved past the "which model to use" question and into "how to optimize it for my specific use case."

On Chinese models like DeepSeek, Srivastava takes a pragmatic stance: "If you could run DeepSeek at 20% of the cost of running OpenAI or Anthropic models in production with comparable or better latency, probably better reliability — if we don't have access to that intelligence in that form, I think it's just a massive loss. As a country, we won't be able to innovate as fast."

His advice for companies considering custom models: "Go prove to yourself with the best-in-class model that you have something worth optimizing. No post-training pre product-market fit."

On scaling the company itself, Srivastava learned the hard way that flat organizations need leaders. "As engineers, you're like, 'It's all overhead.' But actually having a leadership team you can trust is so important. You want people where you can give them whole problems." His hiring rubric: first-principles thinkers who work hard and are kind — no hero culture, low ego.

The episode closes with a vision of a "concierge for everything" future — personalized AI for healthcare, education, and daily life. "If you don't embrace this," Srivastava warns, "it's the extinction moment for a bunch of folks."

**核心观点：** AI 推理市场是最后一个市场——即使在 AGI 之后，剩下的也只有推理——而且目前正面临大多数人没有充分认识的严重供应短缺。

Baseten CEO Tuhin Srivastava 做客 No Priors，讨论了 AI 推理市场的现状。他的公司过去一年增长了 30 倍，有望突破 10 亿美元收入。Srivastava 的核心论点是：推理是终极市场，因为它代表了智能的持续消费，而不仅仅是其创造。

最引人注目的见解是计算资源短缺的严重性。Srivastava 透露，Baseten 的集群运行在"令人不安的高利用率"下——大部分时间在 95% 左右。该公司横跨 18 个不同的云服务商，在全球拥有 90 个集群，但仍难找到供应。现在要获得新容量，需要签订 3-5 年的合同，并预付 20-30%。"可用的备用计算资源非常非常少，"他说。"即使有可用容量，也有很多不靠谱的供应商，他们不了解推理的 SLA。大概只有十几个好的云服务商，我会把其中三四个放在黄金级别。"

关于应用层之争——独立 AI 公司能否在实验室面前生存——Srivastava 持乐观态度。他的论点是：价值在于只有应用层才能捕获的用户信号和工作流程。他以 Abridge 为例：价值不仅在于转录，还在于笔记完成后 EMR 内部的三步工作流程。"前沿模型公司很难吃掉那个市场——他们根本没有访问那个用户信号的途径。"

一个令人惊讶的数据点：Baseten 上服务的 90-95% 的 token 来自定制模型，而不是普通的开源模型。"没有人只是运行普通的开源权重，"Srivastava 说。"你可能为了质量而定制它，但你也可能为了性能而定制它。"这表明市场已经超越了"使用哪个模型"的问题，进入了"如何针对我的特定用例优化它"的阶段。

关于像 DeepSeek 这样的中国模型，Srivastava 采取了务实的态度："如果你能以 OpenAI 或 Anthropic 模型生产成本的 20% 来运行 DeepSeek，且延迟相当或更好，可靠性可能更高——如果我们无法以那种形式获得那种智能，我认为这是一个巨大的损失。作为一个国家，我们将无法那么快地创新。"

他给考虑定制模型的公司的建议是："先用最先进的模型向自己证明，你有值得优化的东西。在产品与市场匹配之前，不要做后训练。"

关于公司自身的规模化，Srivastava 通过艰难的方式学到，扁平化组织需要领导者。"作为工程师，你会说，'那都是 overhead。'但实际上，拥有一个你可以信任的领导团队非常重要。你需要那些你可以把整个问题交给他们的人。"他的招聘标准是：从第一性原理思考、努力工作且友善的人——没有英雄文化，低 ego。

该集以对"万物管家"未来的展望结束——为医疗保健、教育和日常生活提供个性化 AI。"如果你不接受这一点，"Srivastava 警告说，"对很多人来说，这就是灭绝的时刻。"
