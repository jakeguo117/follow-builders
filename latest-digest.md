### The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — OpenAI's Dan Roberts: Why AI Can Now Make Discoveries

**The Takeaway:** Reinforcement learning is no longer the cherry on top — it's the cake. The combination of powerful pretrained language models with RL at test time is what's producing genuine scientific discoveries, including solving long-standing math problems.

Dan Roberts leads the Foundations of Reinforcement Learning team at OpenAI. He came from theoretical physics (PhD from MIT, postdoc at the Institute for Advanced Study), spent time at FAIR and Sequoia Capital, and co-authored *The Principles of Deep Learning Theory*. His background makes him uniquely positioned to discuss the intersection of physics, RL, and scientific discovery.

The conversation centers on a remarkable week where OpenAI, DeepMind, and Anthropic all cracked unsolved Erdős problems in mathematics. But the really interesting part is *how* they did it — and the approaches couldn't be more different.

OpenAI's approach was informal reasoning: they gave the model the problem in plain English and let it think at test time, producing a chain of thought that could run for hours. DeepMind's approach was formal: they translated problems into the Lean proof assistant and searched for proofs there. Both worked, but they represent fundamentally different philosophies about how AI should do science.

Roberts explains that the key insight behind RL working now is that you need a powerful enough pretrained model first. "If you have a powerful enough pretrained model, then it can start to do well at RL." The pretrained model provides an incredibly rich prior — all of human knowledge encoded in language — and RL then teaches it to *use* that knowledge through extended reasoning.

He pushes back hard on the idea that "scale is all you need." "I have a somewhat contrarian take on the bitter lesson — it's not that scale is all you need. You need to also have good ideas to guide the scaling." The interplay between scaling and new ideas (like applying RL to pretrained models) is what produces breakthroughs.

Perhaps the most memorable line comes from Roberts' description of what makes the OpenAI Erdős result special: "One of the things that ChatGPT was able to do was assume it was false. When you go against the grain and do something contrarian like that, you really have to have strong conviction in what you're doing in order to persevere down a really long calculation path."

On the question of whether AI is doing original science: Roberts is already convinced. The unit distance problem required the model to be contrarian, persistent, and draw on disparate fields of mathematics — a combination unlikely to be found in any single human.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oWOz2htozfI

**核心观点：** 强化学习不再是蛋糕上的樱桃——它本身就是蛋糕。强大的预训练语言模型与测试时的 RL 相结合，正在产生真正的科学发现，包括解决长期悬而未决的数学问题。

Dan Roberts 领导 OpenAI 的强化学习基础团队。他拥有理论物理学背景（MIT 博士，高等研究院博士后），曾在 FAIR 和红杉资本工作，并合著了《深度学习理论原理》一书。他的背景使他能够独特地讨论物理学、RL 和科学发现之间的交叉点。

对话围绕 OpenAI、DeepMind 和 Anthropic 都攻克了未解决的 Erdős 数学问题这一非凡的一周展开。但真正有趣的是它们*如何*做到的——方法截然不同。

OpenAI 的方法是 informal reasoning：用自然语言给模型问题，让它在测试时思考，产生可以运行数小时的思维链。DeepMind 的方法是 formal：将问题翻译成 Lean 证明助手，在那里搜索证明。两者都有效，但代表了关于 AI 应如何做科学的根本不同理念。

Roberts 解释说，RL 现在能起作用的关键是首先需要一个足够强大的预训练模型。"如果你有一个足够强大的预训练模型，它就能在 RL 中表现良好。"预训练模型提供了一个极其丰富的先验——所有人类知识以语言形式编码——然后 RL 教它通过扩展推理来*使用*这些知识。

他强烈反驳"规模就是一切"的观点。"我对 bitter lesson 有一个有点反主流的看法——规模不是一切。你还需要好的想法来指导规模化。"规模和新想法（如将 RL 应用于预训练模型）之间的相互作用才是产生突破的关键。

最令人难忘的一句话来自 Roberts 描述 OpenAI 的 Erdős 结果为何特别："ChatGPT 能够做的事情之一是假设它是错的。当你逆流而行，做这种反常规的事情时，你真的需要有强烈的信念，才能坚持走完一条非常长的计算路径。"

关于 AI 是否在做原创科学的问题：Roberts 已经相信了。单位距离问题需要模型反常规、坚持不懈，并利用不同数学领域的知识——这是一个在任何单个人类身上都难以找到的组合。
